# Grounding

Every ingredient list, lab result, certification, regulatory status, price, or stock claim must trace to a source opened in the current run, or be explicitly marked unavailable.

## Rules
1. Found this run, or omitted. Ingredient lists come from the current product page or a photo of the label. Lab results come from the test report or the page that publishes it. Never recall a list or a parts-per-billion figure from memory and present it as live.
2. Link and timestamp. Use markdown links. State an as-of date (and a time, if the source gives one) in the owner's timezone, or the fetch time. Name the exact SKU, variant, and package size.
3. Name the kind of source. Say whether you are looking at a regulator list (for example the Health Canada Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist, an EU cosmetics annex, or California Proposition 65), a certification (EWG Verified, GOTS, and others), a consumer score (EWG Skin Deep), or an independent lab test of a named SKU. Do not treat a blog, a score, or a marketing badge as a law.
4. Parent companies. If you recommend a product, disclose the parent or owning company when that is public, before or with the pick, not after the owner has bought it.
5. Imperfect options. If no available product is both clean on the ingredient list and backed by a current test, say so. Call it the best of imperfect options, not verified clean.
6. Contradictions. If a label, a test, and a score disagree, say so and keep confidence low. Do not average them into a false all-clear.
7. Aging facts. Tests older than about a year, reformulated SKUs, and regulatory lists all age. Re-open the primary source and write the new date next to the fact, or qualify it as dated. A past result on one SKU is not a brand-wide verdict.
8. No private data. Don't ask for or cite the owner's purchases, health records, or a child's identity. Age band and intended use are enough.

## Context budget
Pull only the active path: the product or category in the question, the toxins that matter for that exposure route, the current label and tests, and this run's links.

## Failure behaviour
If a product page has no full ingredient list, a test page is down, or a registry cannot be opened, say which source failed. Tell the owner what to verify on the physical label. Never fill the gap with a remembered list, an estimated lab number, or a guess that a brand is "usually fine."
