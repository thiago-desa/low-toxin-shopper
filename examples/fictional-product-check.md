## Imagined answer

Paste is optional at this age. Water on a soft brush or a clean cloth can be enough, especially if some of the paste will be swallowed. If you still want a paste, the chemicals I would watch in this use are SLS, dyes, titanium dioxide, undeclared fragrance, and heavy metals in mineral or clay formulas.

I opened the Cedar Sprout Berry 40 g listing this run (fetched 25 November 2026, America/Vancouver). The ingredient list on that page starts with water and xylitol and does not list SLS, dyes, or titanium dioxide. I could not find a current independent heavy-metal test for this exact SKU. A June 2025 report on a different Cedar Sprout flavor is not a result for Berry, so I am not reusing those numbers.

Cedar Sprout is a made-up independent brand in this example. If a real pick were owned by a larger company, that parent would be named here, before the recommendation.

A cleaner-looking fictional alternative, "Northshore Baby Toothpaste, Strawberry, 40 g," has a short, fragrance-free list on its 25 November 2026 product page and a March 2026 independent test of that same SKU that reported non-detect for lead, arsenic, cadmium, and mercury. That test is now several months old, so I would still treat it as dated and re-check before you rely on it. I have not found a fluoride version of Northshore. If you and your child's clinician want a fluoride paste, I have not found an option in this fictional set that has both a short dye-free list and a current heavy-metal test. That would be a best-of-imperfect choice, not a verified-clean one.

What to verify on the tube: the flavor and size match the listing, the ingredient list matches what you were shown, and there is no "fragrance" or SLS further down the pack copy. I would not heat or store this in a way the label warns against, and I would use only a rice-grain smear if you use paste at all.
